Slide 11 defines similarity as the quality or state of being similar, likened to resemblance. It notes that while we recognize similarity intuitively ("We know it when we see it"), its real meaning is philosophical, and a pragmatic approach is taken instead. The slide introduces defining distance measures: for objects O₁ and O₂ from the universe of possible objects, the distance (dissimilarity) D(O₁,O₂) is a real number. Examples include gene1 and gene2, with outputs like 0.23, 3, and 342.7 shown via black boxes representing functions on two variables—some simple, some very complex. Key properties a distance measure should have are listed: Symmetry [D(A,B) = D(B,A)], Constancy of Self-Similarity [D(A,B) = 0 iff A = B], Positivity [D(A,B) ≥ 0], and Triangular Inequality [D(A,B) ≤ D(A,C) + D(B,C)].
